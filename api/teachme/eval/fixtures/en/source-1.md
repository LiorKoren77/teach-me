# The Water Cycle

Water is never created or destroyed on Earth. The same water has been moving between the ocean,
the atmosphere, the land and living things for billions of years. That endless movement is called
the water cycle, and it is driven almost entirely by energy from the Sun together with the pull of
gravity.

## Evaporation

The cycle begins wherever the Sun warms water. Heat from sunlight gives molecules at the surface of
an ocean, a lake or a puddle enough energy to break away from the liquid and rise into the air as
water vapour, an invisible gas. This change from liquid to gas is called evaporation. Roughly nine
tenths of the vapour in the atmosphere evaporates from the oceans, because the oceans cover most of
the planet's surface and absorb most of its sunlight.

Plants add to that supply. Roots draw water out of the soil, the water travels up through stems and
leaves, and it escapes through tiny pores on the underside of each leaf. This is called
transpiration. Together, evaporation and transpiration are often described as one process,
evapotranspiration, because from a distance a forest and a lake both simply hand water to the air.

## Condensation and clouds

Air cools as it rises, because pressure falls with height and rising air expands. Cool air can hold
less vapour than warm air, so at some height the vapour begins to change back into liquid. It does
this on the surface of microscopic particles of dust, salt and smoke floating in the atmosphere,
called condensation nuclei. Each droplet is far too small to fall; a cloud is simply an enormous
number of these droplets held up by rising air. Near the ground the same process makes fog, dew on
grass, and mist on a cold window.
